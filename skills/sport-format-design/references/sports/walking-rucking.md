# Walking and rucking reference

## Formats that work
| Format | Shape | Length | Good size |
|--------|-------|--------|-----------|
| City walk with stops (neighborhood, murals, music history) | Together | 60 to 120 min | 15 to 200 |
| Ruck march (weighted pack) | Together | 3 to 12 mi | 15 to 150 |
| Walk/roll 5K alongside a run | Together | 60 to 90 min | Any |
| Step challenge ending in a group walk | Challenge window | 1 to 4 weeks + event | Any |

## Levels and adaptations
- **Open door:** no pack, short loop, frequent stops.
- **Steady:** standard route, light pack optional.
- **Push:** longer route or heavier pack.
- **Adaptive:** paved, step-free route with seating at stops; wheelchair and stroller friendly when surface allows.
- **Ruck weight guidance (ASSUMPTION):** start light. Many beginner programs suggest starting around 10% of bodyweight or less, and never require a weight. Let people choose.

## Venue and permission
- Sidewalks and public paths often need no permit for small groups; promoted events and large groups usually do. Ask the city about special events thresholds.
- Stops at private businesses need the owner's OK.

## Equipment
- Producer: first aid with blister supplies, water, route map, a few loaner packs if rucking.
- Participant: shoes, water, pack if rucking.

## Safety specifics
- Blisters, hot spots, and heat are the common problems. Stop early, stop often.
- Ruck: hip belt and fit matter more than weight; drop weight if form breaks.

## Scoring and standards
- Collective miles or collective pounds carried is a great shared total.

## The artist moment
- Tells the story at a stop that matters to them. A song at the end point.

## The connection moment
- Walking is the best format for conversation. Assign walking partners for the first stretch.

## Natural partner categories
Footwear, packs and outdoor gear, local businesses on the route, health systems, coffee, hydration.

## Content moments
Group at a landmark stop, packs lined up, the artist talking at a stop, the finish.
