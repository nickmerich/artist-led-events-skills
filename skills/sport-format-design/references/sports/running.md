# Running reference

## Formats that work
| Format | Shape | Length | Good size |
|--------|-------|--------|-----------|
| Community group run (3 mi / 5 mi / 10K options) | Together | 60 to 90 min incl. hang | 15 to 150 |
| Artist 5K (untimed or optional timing) | Together | 2 to 3 hours on site | 100 to 1,000 |
| Shakeout before a marathon or race weekend | Together | 45 to 75 min | 30 to 300 |
| Conference or side-event morning run | Together | 60 to 90 min, finish early enough for attendees to shower | 20 to 150 |
| Four-week fan challenge ending in a group run | Challenge window | 4 weeks + event | Any |

## Levels and adaptations
- **Open door:** a walk group with its own leader and the same start time. Run/walk is welcome in every group.
- **Steady:** conversational pace groups with named leaders.
- **Push:** a faster group or a longer route option. Never the only group that gets the artist.
- **Adaptive:** walk/roll on a paved or crushed surface route; confirm surface and curb cuts in advance.
- **Out-and-back routes** let every group turn around at their distance and finish together. Best design for mixed crowds.

## Venue and permission
- **Typical venues:** trails, parks, closed or quiet roads, tracks.
- **Who grants it:** trail councils, township or city parks and recreation, police or public works for road use. Trails can split authority by section; confirm who owns each segment.
- **Lead time:** informal group runs often need none below certain size and activity triggers. Promoted events commonly need 6 to 12 weeks or more. Some trail owners require a nonprofit requester, limit organized events per month, or ban sales on the trail. Ask.
- **Common requirements:** COI naming the owner as additional insured, a route map, cleanup plan, restroom plan, and limits on giveaways or cups (bottle-refill stations may be the only allowed water format).

## Staffing starting points (ASSUMPTION; confirm with insurer and medical lead)
| Role | Starting ratio | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Pace group leader | 1 per 10 to 15 runners | Named, visible, knows the route |
| Sweep | 1 per route option | Last person, carries phone and first aid |
| Course marshal | Every turn, crossing, or confusing junction | Crossings need extra |
| Check-in | 1 per 75 arrivals in the peak 20 minutes | QR check-in speeds this up |
| Medical | Named lead for every event; scale with size and distance | Ask local EMS for guidance above 250 participants |
| Welcome / first-timer host | 1 per 20 expected first-timers | Learns names, pairs people up |

## Equipment
- Producer: route signage or flags, cones, first aid kit, AED access plan, water (bottle refill if required), bib or numbered card if used, sound for start and post-run.
- Participant: shoes, water bottle, weather layers.

## Safety specifics
- Heat and lightning plans with written thresholds (see `permits-insurance-and-risk`).
- Dark mornings and evenings: lights, reflective gear, and a no-headphones-at-crossings rule.
- Road crossings are the highest-risk points. Staff every one.
- Minors: require a guardian on site and a guardian-signed waiver.

## Scoring and standards
- Default untimed. If timing, keep an untimed option and never post a finisher leaderboard without consent.
- Numbered tickets can mark belonging (001 of 100), not finish order.

## The artist moment
- Starts the run, runs a section with each group, or is the final sweep.
- Post-run set, listening session, or one unreleased song on the speaker.

## The connection moment
- Pace groups with leaders who do names in the first minute.
- Post-run coffee or food table. People stay when there's a reason to.

## Natural partner categories
Running shoes and apparel, hydration, coffee, nutrition, recovery, audio (open-ear headphones), local running stores, health systems, physical therapy.

## Content moments
The start, groups strung out on the route, high-fives at the turnaround, the finish arch or line of crew, the post-run hang.

## Sanctioning bodies or insurers worth knowing
Road Runners Club of America (club insurance and a group music license program), USA Track & Field (race sanctioning). Your broker may require one.
