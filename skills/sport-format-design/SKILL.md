# Sport Format Design

A good format answers one question for every person who shows up: **what am I going to do, and who am I doing it with?**

## Step 1: Load the sport reference

Open `references/sports/<sport>.md`. If the sport isn't there, copy `references/sports/_new-sport-template.md`, fill it in with the user, and mark every unknown.

Available: `running`, `walking-rucking`, `cycling`, `basketball`, `soccer`, `weighted-carries`, `pull-ups`.

## Step 2: Pick the format shape

Every artist-led event fits one of five shapes. Choose one; hybrids get messy.

| Shape | What it is | Best when |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Together** | Everyone does the same thing at once, in groups by level. Group run, social ride, city walk. | Big mixed crowds, first events, artist wants to be in the middle of it. |
| **Collective total** | The crowd works toward one shared number. 500 pull-ups, 100 miles carried, 1,000 made shots. | Strength and skill sports; makes every rep count no matter the level. |
| **Stations** | Small groups rotate through 3 to 5 stations. | Skill sports, mixed abilities, limited space. |
| **Games** | Small-sided pickup games, rotating teams. | Basketball, soccer; artist plays a few games, not all of them. |
| **Challenge window** | A one- to four-week self-paced challenge that ends with an in-person day. | Building a habit, spreading out across cities, artist checks in weekly. |

## Step 3: Write the format brief

Use `assets/format-brief.md`. Required sections:

1. **One-line format:** what happens, how long, for whom.
2. **Levels and adaptations:** at least three ways to take part. One must need no prior experience and no equipment. Include walk/roll or seated options where the sport allows.
3. **Capacity:** the smallest of venue capacity, staffing capacity, permit limit, and insurance limit. Show all four.
4. **Minute-by-minute outline** from arrival to last person leaving (detailed run of show comes later in `run-of-show-and-staffing`).
5. **The artist moment:** one or two specific, low-effort moments. Starting the run, leading the warm-up, playing the post-event set, taking the last rep of the collective total. Never a required photo line.
6. **The connection moment:** how strangers end up talking. Pace groups with named leaders, mixed teams, partner reps, a shared table after.
7. **Scoring or completion:** collective, not ranked, unless the artist and the crowd specifically want competition. If ranked, keep an unranked option equal in status.
8. **Staffing starting points** from the sport reference, labeled ASSUMPTION until confirmed with the insurer, venue, and medical lead.
9. **Equipment and venue needs.**
10. **What we don't do:** VIP sections, fastest-finisher spotlight, "elite only" heats, anything that makes the back of the pack feel like an afterthought.

## Step 4: Check the doorway

Before finishing, answer yes to all:

- Could a parent who hasn't exercised in two years finish this and feel good?
- Could a former college athlete find it hard enough to care?
- Does every participant interact with at least two people they didn't come with?
- If the artist got sick that morning, is the event still worth showing up for?

If any answer is no, redesign.

## Capacity math

```
capacity = min(venue_limit, permit_limit, insurance_limit, staff_count × participants_per_staff)
```

Use the sport reference's participants-per-staff starting point. Plan registration at capacity; plan the day for a show rate you can justify (see `registration-and-capacity`).

## Output

A completed format brief, the capacity calculation with sources, and a list of UNKNOWNs with who can answer each.
