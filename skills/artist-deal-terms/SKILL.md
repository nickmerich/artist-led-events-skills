# Artist Deal Terms

A clear deal protects the artist's time and name, and protects the producer from building an event around a maybe. Draft the term sheet; a lawyer finalizes the agreement.

## Step 1: Pick the ask size

Smaller asks get faster yeses. Offer two shapes:

- **Low-lift:** one morning, about 90 minutes on site, one point of contact, no promotion required. Private or small public.
- **Full:** on-site role plus a set number of promotional posts and one pre-event content moment.

## Step 2: Choose the money model

| Model | How it works | Use when |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| **Profit share after costs** | Agreed costs come off the top; profit splits by an agreed ratio (50/50 is a clean default). | Paid entry or meaningful partner revenue; both sides want upside. |
| **Flat fee** | Fixed appearance or performance fee. | Artist needs certainty; event is partner-funded. |
| **Fee + share** | Smaller guaranteed fee plus share above break-even. | Larger events. |
| **No fee, value exchange** | Content, audience growth, cause alignment, product. | Small pilots, community events, artist's own idea. |

Rules:
- Split **profit after agreed costs**, not gross. List the cost categories in the term sheet so nobody argues later.
- Keep **talent compensation separate from event production funding.** A partner funding coffee and permits isn't paying the artist, and the term sheet should say which money is which.
- Settlement date and statement: within an agreed number of days after the event, with a line-item statement.

## Step 3: Rights and approvals (the part people skip)

- **Name, likeness, logos, marks:** list exactly which uses are approved (event title, poster, social posts, merch). Merch needs its own approval.
- **Approval rights:** artist approves the announcement, event title, and any partner associated with their name. Set a response window (for example, 48 hours) and a default if no answer.
- **Music:** recorded music played at the event needs public performance licensing, and the event owner is responsible, not the DJ or artist (see `permits-insurance-and-risk`). Music in published video needs sync rights from the publisher and label. Artist-owned masters and publishing simplify this; confirm who owns what.
- **Content:** who can capture, who owns footage, who can post, approval for posts featuring the artist, usage window, and whether partners can reuse it (usually requires extra permission and sometimes payment).
- **Unreleased music:** if previewed, no recording; say how that's enforced.

## Step 4: Conflicts and exclusivities

Before any partner is pitched with the artist's name:
- Ask for the artist's active brand deals and category exclusivities.
- Run every partner against that list. Conflicts beat the original sponsor list; replace the category, don't stall the event.
- Note sensitive topics or substances. If the artist's brand involves anything not legal or appropriate at a public athletic event, keep the event footprint clean and move any adult or licensed component to a separate, legal, controlled setting after the athletic portion.

## Step 5: Safety and conduct

- Artist participates under the same waiver as everyone, or a separate talent agreement covers it.
- Talent protection: arrival and exit route, one handler, no guaranteed photo line, a hard stop time.
- Producer owns safety decisions, including weather calls. The artist doesn't override them.

## Step 6: Cancellation

- Weather or safety cancellation: who pays what, reschedule window.
- Artist cancellation: replacement artist allowed? Refund policy for paid entry?
- Producer cancellation: costs already incurred.

## Output

Fill `assets/term-sheet.md`. Mark every term as AGREED (with the message or doc that shows it), PROPOSED, or OPEN. A proposal sent is not agreement.
