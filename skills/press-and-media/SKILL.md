# Press and Media

## Step 1: Build the proof table first

Before any writing, list every claim the release might make and label it.

| Claim type | Allowed when | Otherwise write |
|---|---|---|
| Signups and finishers | Organizer-recorded counts exist | "about N," or leave out |
| Artist involvement | Written in the deal terms | Name the album or the inspiration, never say the artist attended or ran |
| Artist or participant quote | That person approved the exact wording in writing | Placeholder, or no quote |
| Partner or sponsor | Signed agreement and written OK for press use | "supported with in-kind [product] at [event]" or omit |
| Future event | Permit, venue, and date confirmed in writing | "planning a return in [month]" |
| First-time or demographic stats | Collected at registration with consent | Omit |

Signups, check-ins, and finishers are three numbers. Use the one you can defend.

## Step 2: One angle per outlet type

| Outlet type | Angle | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Local news | A neighborhood did a thing; photos; a date people can attend | Who, when, where, how to join, one cleared photo |
| Music press | The artist's work became a physical experience | A cleared artist quote, the album or project, a link to the artist's own page |
| Running press | The format: free, small, permit-approved, who showed up | Counts, course, what made it different from a race |
| Business or tech press | The producer's model: how it is funded, what it costs, what is reusable | Economics you are willing to publish, the repeatable system |
| National trend | The counterexample to whatever is being covered this month | A link to the trend story, one sentence on why yours is the other end of it |

Pitching a music outlet without a cleared artist quote wastes the first contact. Clear the quote first.

## Step 3: Find the outlet's stated route

Fetch the outlet's contact, about, or pitch page and use what it says: a tips inbox, a form, a named desk editor, or "reply to the newsletter." Record the URL you took it from. If a page states no route, pitch a reporter from a recent byline on a similar story. Never guess an address pattern; a bounce is a lost outlet.

## Step 4: Write the release and the note

- Headline is the story, not the organization. "When the album became a 5K" beats "[Org] hosts run."
- First paragraph: what happened, where, how many, when it happens again.
- Second: the artist connection and the community, with only cleared quotes.
- Third: who produced it, what it costs to join, how to join.
- Boilerplate: one paragraph, one link, one working press contact that has been tested with an external send.
- The cover note is four sentences, personalized to one recent piece the recipient wrote, and offers one specific thing: a visit, a call, photos, or a date.

## Step 5: Send in waves tied to real dates

A wave needs a reason: an event in seven to ten days, a result from the weekend, a confirmed date, or a cleared quote. A release with no new reason is noise. Follow up once, seven days later, with new information. Then stop.

## Step 6: Podcasts are a separate channel

Podcast hosts reply more often than reporters. Pitch with one recent episode named, one guest angle, and a second community voice offered. When a host replies with a scheduling question, answer the same day. An unanswered "Zoom or phone?" is a lost recording.

## Track replies, not sends

| Count | Means |
|---|---|
| Sent | Activity. Report it, do not celebrate it. |
| Delivered | Not bounced. Still activity. |
| Replied | The first real signal |
| Scheduled | A recording, call, or visit on the calendar |
| Published, organization named | Coverage |
| Published, organization not named | A listing. Do not call it a feature. |

Keep a one-line log per outlet: date, route used, source URL for the route, what was sent, reply status, next date. Report counts outside the team; keep contact details inside it.

## Do not

- Invent a founder quote and label it approved.
- Reuse a quote from your own website as press-cleared. Website presence is not press permission.
- Call a calendar listing a feature, or a brief a profile.
- Send the same release to the same outlet twice.
- Name children in photos, or any participant without consent.
